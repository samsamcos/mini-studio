What it is

The editing machine you run at home

Mini Studio is a self-hosted AI video editing server. It runs as a VM or mini-PC on your local network, processes your raw recordings overnight or while you work, and delivers a fully-assembled OpenCut project you can open and finalise in minutes.

The problem it solves

Recording a video takes 30 minutes. Editing it used to take 2–3 hours: trimming dead air, removing bad takes, picking the best moments, writing a script, recording a voice-over, timing captions, exporting for Shorts. Most creators either skip these steps or spend more time editing than creating.

Mini Studio compresses that 2–3 hours of mechanical work into a 15-minute automated job. You still make every creative decision — you just make them on a finished edit rather than on raw footage.

What you actually do

Drop the file in. Pick a channel. Pick a character voice. Press one button. Come back when it's done. Open the timeline in OpenCut, watch it through, move anything you don't like, and export. That's the entire workflow.

The 2% that's yours is exactly what editing should be: creative judgement. Does this clip feel right? Is this the best thumbnail? Should the chapter start here or 10 seconds earlier? Everything mechanical was already done.

The Pipeline

14 stages, one button

Every stage runs automatically and in sequence. A failure in any single stage is caught, logged as a warning, and the pipeline continues — you always get usable output at the end, even if one step had a problem.

01 — PROBE
🔍

File Analysis

ffprobe reads duration, resolution, bitrate, and codec. 4K/64 Mbps sources are handled throughout — preview clips are capped at 1080p but the final timeline always references your original file at full quality.

02 — TRANSCRIBE
🎙️

Word-Level Transcript

faster-whisper (small, int8, CPU) with VAD filter and word timestamps. Every word gets its own start and end time. These timings drive captions, scoring, bad-take detection, and karaoke word animation — all four consumers share one transcription pass.

03 — DEAD AIR
✂️

Silence Removal

ffmpeg silencedetect finds gaps below −32 dB lasting 0.55 s or more. A 0.18 s pad is applied on each side when inverting to speaking ranges — preserving breath pauses and natural rhythm rather than creating choppy fragments. Typically removes 3–5 minutes from a 20-minute vlog.

04 — BAD TAKES
🚩

Take Detection

Five patterns: repeated words within a 3-word window, immediate stutters, filler rate above 28%, explicit restart phrases ("let me start again"), and sub-1.5 s fragments under 4 words. Bad take windows are excluded from scoring and appear as red markers in the timeline.

05 — LOUDNESS
📊

Energy Map

ffmpeg ebur128 measures momentary loudness every 0.1 s across the full recording. Produces the time-indexed energy array the scorer uses. The top-half averaging technique means pauses don't unfairly penalise high-energy windows.

06 — HIGHLIGHTS
⭐

Best Moments

Sliding windows scored: 0.45 × z(loudness) + 0.35 × z(speech density) + 0.20 × keyword hits. Winners picked greedily, no overlaps, until the keep-% budget runs out. Every picked clip has a pre-computed alternate — swap any clip with one click, no re-analysis.

07 — SCRIPT
✍️

Source-Grounded Polish

Gemma 2 9B cleans the transcript at temperature 0.1 with strict rules: no invented facts, no added narrative, stay inside the source text. Word ceiling = (clip duration − 1 s) × 2.3 wps — matched to Pocket TTS's actual speaking rate.

08 — TTS
🗣️

AI Voice

Pocket TTS (Kyutai Moshi) speaks the script in the character's voice. 3.3× realtime on CPU — 10 seconds of audio in ~3 seconds. Runs entirely on your hardware, no external API call for the voice itself.

09 — AUDIO
🔊

Clean & Normalise

Three-stage chain: highpass 80 Hz (removes rumble/HVAC), afftdn neural noise suppression (removes hiss and room tone), loudnorm I=−16 TP=−1.5 (broadcast-standard loudness for YouTube/streaming). Output: narration_clean.m4a.

10 — FIT
⏱️

Timing Check

If narration overruns the footage, the last video clip's trimEnd is extended to cover it plus a 0.5 s tail pad. Footage keeps playing rather than audio over a black frame. No re-encoding — this is a timeline JSON edit only.

11 — CAPTIONS
💬

Timed Captions

Whisper word timestamps rebased onto the cut timeline (not the source). SRT for universal compatibility, styled ASS with the character's font/colour/outline for burn-in workflows. Timeline carries wordTimings arrays for OpenCut's karaoke animation.

12 — CHAPTERS
📑

Chapter Markers

Speech gaps longer than 6 s become chapter boundaries, up to 12 chapters. First few words of the following segment become the chapter title. chapters.txt is YouTube-format — paste it directly into your video description.

13 — THUMBNAILS
🖼️

Frame Candidates

ffmpeg thumbnail filter scans the full source and picks the 6 most visually distinct frames using scene-change scoring. You choose the best one — the pipeline doesn't make that creative call for you.

14 — TIMELINE
🎞️

OpenCut Project

All outputs assembled into timeline.json. Video clips reference the original file with trimStart/trimEnd — drag any handle longer in OpenCut without re-encoding. Bad takes are red markers. Captions are a separate track with per-word timing. 9:16 vertical cut included.

Features

Every piece connects

Mini Studio is not a collection of individual scripts. Every stage feeds the next. The transcript drives the scoring, the scoring drives the clips, the clips drive the script, the script drives the TTS, the TTS drives the captions. One pipeline, one output.

🎭

Character Profiles

JARVIS (corporate, precise), ARIA (warm, energetic), Narrator (neutral documentary). Each carries a voice, narration tone, caption font/size/colour/outline, zoom frequency, and vertical crop mode. Build your own with a single API call.

📺

Channel Profiles

Per-channel editing dials stored in SQLite. Silence threshold, cut speed, caption style, TTS pace, zoom frequency, music mood. Set them once per channel — every subsequent job for that channel starts from your preferred settings rather than the defaults.

🔄

Non-Destructive Editing

Timeline clips reference the original file with trimStart/trimEnd values. Drag any cut longer in OpenCut without re-encoding or re-running anything. 4K sources stay 4K in the edit. Preview clips are 1080p just for reviewability.

🔁

One-Click Clip Swap

Every highlight has a pre-computed alternate — the next-best non-overlapping window. Don't like clip 3? One click replaces it with its alternate. No re-analysis, no waiting. Swap as many times as there are alternates.

📱

9:16 Vertical Automatically

Centre-crop or blur-background modes. The vertical cut is generated in the same job as the widescreen edit. No extra step, no separate run. Open the vertical.mp4 and the reel.mp4 from the same output folder.

🧠

On-Demand AI Models

Gemma 2 9B and Qwen 2.5 3B are disabled at boot. They start on the first request that needs them and stop after 10 minutes idle. Idle RAM: ~0.8 GB. Both loaded: ~7.3 GB. Your machine stays usable between jobs.

🛡️

Fault-Tolerant Pipeline

Every stage is wrapped in a soft() handler. A Gemini quota hit, TTS timeout, or ffmpeg codec error gets recorded in job warnings and the pipeline carries on. You always get something useful at the end — never a blank folder.

⚡

Zero GPU Required

Runs entirely on CPU. Whisper small int8, Gemma 2 9B Q4_K_M, and Pocket TTS all perform acceptably on a standard mini-PC or VM. No CUDA, no ROCm, no driver headaches.

🎯

Source-Grounded Script

Temperature 0.1, five strict rules, word ceiling derived from clip duration. Gemma cleans what was said — it doesn't invent new narrative. Nothing appears in the output that wasn't in the source transcript.

💬

Karaoke Captions

Per-word timing from Whisper drives word-pop animation in OpenCut. Each caption element carries a wordTimings array so the active word highlights as the narration plays — no manual timing, no sync work.

📡

Full REST API

Every service exposes a clean REST API. Start jobs, poll status, download outputs, manage characters, update channel profiles — all scriptable. Build your own integrations or trigger jobs from other tools.

🔒

Fully Self-Hosted

Your footage never leaves your network. The only external calls are optional Gemini vision tagging (480p/1fps proxy, deleted after each run) and Groq for STT fallback. Everything else runs locally.

Adding your own character

One API call creates a new character with any voice, tone, and caption style. It's available immediately on the next job.

  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{
    "label": "My Channel",
    "voice": "http://YOUR-STUDIO-IP:5020/voices/custom",
    "tone": "casual and direct",

API Reference

Every service is scriptable

All endpoints accept and return JSON. No authentication required on the local network. Every service runs on its own port so you can call individual stages independently.

POST /auto — Start an Auto-Edit job

Starts the full 14-stage pipeline. Returns immediately with a job ID. Poll /auto/<id> for progress.

{
  "video_path":  "/opt/studio/incoming/recording.mp4",
  "character":   "narrator",     // jarvis | aria | narrator | custom
  "percent":     2,              // % of total duration to keep
  "clip_seconds": 8,             // target clip length
  "max_clips":   8,              // hard cap on clip count
  "tts":         true,           // generate AI narration
  "narrate":     true,           // polish the script first
  "vertical":    true            // produce 9:16 version
}
GET /auto/<job_id> — Poll job status

Returns full job state including stage, progress %, timing data, and output file names when complete.

{
  "id":                "20260901-014958-c848",
  "stage":             "done",
  "progress":          100,
  "duration":          1243.0,
  "kept_seconds":      24.8,
  "kept_percent":      2.0,
  "narration_seconds": 9.4,
  "dead_air_seconds":  214.3,
  "bad_takes":         [...],
  "chapters":          [...],
  "reel":              "reel.mp4",
  "vertical":          "vertical.mp4",
  "timeline":          "timeline.json",
  "warnings":          []
}
POST /highlight — Highlights only

Runs just the scoring and clip extraction — no script, no TTS, no timeline. Use this when you want the clips without the full production chain. Every picked clip has a pre-computed alternate for swapping.

{
  "video_path":     "/opt/studio/incoming/recording.mp4",
  "percent":        2,
  "clip_seconds":   8,
  "stride_seconds": 2,
  "max_clips":      8,
  "use_gemini":     false,
  "keywords":       ["tutorial", "tip", "important"]
}
POST /highlight/<id>/swap — Swap a clip

Replaces clip N with its pre-computed alternate and rebuilds the reel. No re-analysis needed.

{"index": 2}   // zero-based clip index
POST /polish — Script polish only

Cleans rough transcript text into narration. Accepts a duration hint to set the word budget. Gemma 2 9B starts on demand if not already loaded.

{
  "raw_script":       "um so today we're gonna talk about...",
  "duration_seconds": 24.8
}
GET /characters  ·  POST /characters/<name>

List all characters or create/update one. Changes take effect on the next job — no restart needed.

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need a GPU?

No. Everything runs on CPU. Whisper small (int8), Gemma 2 9B Q4_K_M, Qwen 2.5 3B Q4_K_M, and Pocket TTS all run acceptably on a standard mini-PC or VM. A dedicated GPU would speed up transcription and TTS but is not required.

How much RAM do I need?

Minimum 12 GB. The system idles at ~0.8 GB. Peak during a job with both LLMs loaded is ~7.3 GB. A 12 GB machine handles this with headroom. 16 GB is comfortable. The on-demand system ensures both models are never loaded simultaneously unless a single job needs both at the same time.

Can it handle 4K footage?

Yes. The pipeline probes the source and works with whatever resolution it finds. Preview clips are capped at 1080p to keep disk usage sensible, but the final timeline.json references the original file. When you export from OpenCut you export at the source resolution.

Will the AI invent things that weren't in my video?

The script polish stage (Gemma 2 9B) runs at temperature 0.1 with five strict rules that forbid inventing facts, adding narrative arcs, or introducing content not in the source transcript. The word ceiling is tied to the clip duration so the output fits the available footage. It cleans what you said — it doesn't write something new.

What if one stage fails?

Every stage is wrapped in a fault-tolerant handler. A failure logs a warning and the pipeline continues with whatever it has. A Gemini quota error means no clip tags. A TTS timeout means no narration. But you still get the transcript, the highlights, the captions, the chapters, and the timeline. Nothing fails silently — warnings are shown in the status panel and stored in the job record.

Can I use my own voice instead of AI TTS?

Yes — set "tts": false when starting the job. The narration track is omitted from the timeline. Source audio is unmuted. You record your voice-over separately and drop it onto the narration track in OpenCut. Captions are still timed and the edit is still assembled — you just provide the voice yourself.

How does the clip swap feature work?

During scoring, for every clip that gets picked, the next-best non-overlapping window is computed and stored as an alternate. If you open the Highlight Extractor and clip 3 looks wrong, one API call or one click in the UI replaces it with its pre-computed alternate and rebuilds the reel. No re-transcription, no re-scoring, no waiting.

Does my footage get uploaded anywhere?

No. Your footage stays on your machine. The only optional external calls are Gemini vision tagging (sent as a 480p/1fps proxy, deleted from Gemini's servers after every run) and Groq for STT fallback. Both are opt-in. Everything else — transcription, scoring, script polish, TTS, ffmpeg processing — runs locally.

What video formats are supported?

Any format that ffmpeg handles, which is essentially everything: MP4, MOV, MKV, AVI, WebM, FLV, and more. Any codec H.264, H.265/HEVC, AV1, VP9, ProRes. Any resolution up to 8K (though very high resolutions slow down the loudness analysis pass). The pipeline has been tested on 4K/60fps H.265 sources.

How do I add a new character voice?

One POST request to /characters/<name> with a JSON body defining the voice URL, narration tone, and caption style. The new character appears immediately in the dropdown on the next page load. No restart required. See the Characters section above for the full example.
